## PART A: PREAMBLE AND STUDENT CHARTER

### PREAMBLE

**A.1 Why this Book exists.** Studying abroad is one of the biggest decisions a student and a family will make. It involves large sums of money, important documents, strict deadlines and decisions made by universities and governments in other countries. Mistakes, false promises and fraud in this field can cost a student years of their life. We have written this Book so that every student knows exactly what we do, how we do it, what we do not do, what the student must do, and what happens if something goes wrong.

**A.2 Who we are.** La Europa Education is the brand of Euroverse Service LLP, an overseas education consultancy based in Surat, Gujarat, India. We assist students with choosing a country and university, applying to universities, and preparing student visa and residence permit applications. We assist with all major study destinations, including Europe, the United Kingdom, North America, Australia, New Zealand, the Middle East and Asia.

**A.3 Our foundation.** We work on the same ethical principles that the international education sector expects of education agents, including the principles of the London Statement and the UK National Code of Ethical Practice for Education Agents: integrity, transparency, confidentiality and professional responsibility towards students, families, Institutions and governments.

**A.4 Our values.** In all our work we follow these values:

(a) **Honesty:** we tell students the truth, including when the truth is that their chances are low.

(b) **Student first:** we suggest what genuinely suits the student's profile, budget and goals.

(c) **Transparency:** we state our fees in writing before work starts, issue receipts for every payment, and are open about our commercial relationships.

(d) **Legality:** we follow the laws of India and of every destination country, and we never use or allow false documents.

(e) **Privacy:** we protect students' personal data and use it only for the purposes the student has agreed to.

(f) **Accountability:** we accept responsibility for our own work and give every student a clear way to complain.

**A.5 Using Part A.** Part A helps explain the meaning of the rest of this Book. If a clause in Part B or Part C is unclear, it should be read in the way that best fits these values. The Student Charter promises below are binding commitments from us, subject to the limits set out in the Rule Book.

**6.2 Blank pages and condition.** Your passport must have enough blank pages and must not be damaged. A damaged passport can be rejected.

**6.3 Renew early.** If your passport will expire within two years, renew it before you apply. Details on your application, test results and admission letter must match your passport exactly.

#### 7. The danger of fake documents

**7.1 What counts as fake.** A fake document is any document that is forged, altered, edited, morphed, back-dated, bought, borrowed from someone else, or issued by an organisation that does not exist or did not really issue it. Examples include edited mark sheets, fake degrees, fake experience letters, fake bank statements, temporary deposits made only to show a balance without a true source, fake sponsor letters, fake language test results and fake medical certificates.

**7.2 How documents are checked.** Institutions and Authorities verify documents with universities, boards, banks, employers and test bodies, and many countries share immigration records. Fake documents are often found, sometimes years later.

**7.3 What can happen.** A fake document or false statement can lead to: refusal; cancellation of admission or visa; a ban on entering one or many countries for many years (in some countries ten years or more); a permanent record in shared immigration databases; criminal prosecution in India under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023 and in the destination country; deportation; and loss of all fees paid.

**7.4 Our rule.** We follow zero tolerance (Rule Book, Chapter 6). If anyone, including any person claiming to be from our office, offers to arrange a document for you, refuse and report it to our Grievance Officer.

#### 8. Money safety and fraud warnings

**8.1 Pay only to official accounts.** Pay our fees only to the bank account of Euroverse Service LLP stated on our official invoice or Portal. Pay Institutions, Authorities and providers only through their own official channels.

**8.2 Check before you pay.** If you receive bank details by message that look different from the details on our invoice or Portal, do not pay. Call our official number to check.

**8.3 Common frauds.** Be careful of: anyone who says a visa or admission can be "arranged" or "guaranteed"; anyone who asks for payment to speed up a decision; fake university offer letters or scholarship offers that ask for fees; fake job offers abroad; and people pretending to be from an embassy, the police or our office who ask for money, OTPs or passwords. We will never ask you for your OTP, banking password or Portal password.

#### 9. Funds and financial documents

**9.1 Lawful funds.** All funds you show must be genuine and from a lawful source that you can explain with documents.

**9.2 Maintenance period.** Some countries require funds to be held for a continuous period (for example a set number of days) before you apply. Moving funds in and out during that period can lead to refusal.

**9.3 Indian foreign exchange rules.** Money sent abroad for education is sent under the Foreign Exchange Management Act 1999 and the Reserve Bank of India's Liberalised Remittance Scheme. Your bank may collect tax at source (TCS) on remittances above the limits set by the Income-tax law in force at the time. The rates and limits change from time to time and depend on whether the payment is funded by an education loan. Ask your bank for the current rate before you remit. TCS collected can usually be adjusted in your income tax return.

**9.4 Loans and providers.** Loans, forex, blocked accounts and insurance are provided only by banks and licensed providers, under their own terms. We may only refer you (Rule Book, Clause 3.10).

#### 10. Health, insurance and accommodation

**10.1 Health insurance.** Most countries require health insurance that meets specific rules. You must buy insurance that meets the Authority's and Institution's published requirements.

**10.2 Medical checks.** Some countries require medical or tuberculosis tests at approved centres. You must attend in person.

**10.3 Accommodation.** You are responsible for finding accommodation. Never pay a deposit to a landlord or website you cannot verify. Accommodation scams are common.

#### 11. Living abroad: follow the rules

**11.1 Visa conditions.** Your visa or permit comes with conditions, such as limits on working hours, attendance requirements, registration with local authorities within a set time, and keeping valid insurance. Breaking them can lead to cancellation of your visa.

**11.2 Local registration.** Many countries require you to register your address and apply for or collect a residence permit soon after arrival. Our pre-departure briefing explains the published steps, but you must complete them yourself.

**11.3 Help in an emergency.** In an emergency abroad, contact local emergency services first. Indian citizens can also contact the nearest Indian Embassy or Consulate, and can raise consular grievances through the Ministry of External Affairs MADAD portal.

#### 12. Do's and Don'ts

**12.1 Do:** tell the truth; keep copies of everything; check every form before submission; pay only official accounts; keep your passport valid; start early; read every message from us and from Institutions; ask when you do not understand.

**12.2 Don't:** use or accept any fake document; hide a previous refusal; apply through another agent without telling us; pay anyone in cash without an official receipt; share your Portal password or OTP; rely on any spoken promise; book non-refundable travel before your visa is granted.

### SCHEDULE 8: STUDENTS UNDER 18

**Schedule 8: Students under 18, Version 1.0, effective 1 January 2027**

This Schedule is part of the La Europa Terms & Conditions (Rule Book).

#### 1. Why this Schedule exists

**1.1 The law.** In India, a person becomes a major at 18 under the Indian Majority Act 1875, and a minor cannot enter into a binding contract under the Indian Contract Act 1872. Under the DPDP Act 2023, a person under 18 is a child, and their data may be processed only with the verifiable consent of a parent or lawful guardian. For these reasons, special rules apply to students under 18.

#### 2. Age rules

**2.1 Under 13.** A person under 13 cannot register on the Portal or use our Services directly. A parent or guardian may contact us for general information only.

**2.2 Age 13 to 17.** A student aged 13 to 17 may register only with the agreement of a parent or lawful guardian under this Schedule.

**2.3 Age check.** We ask every student for their date of birth at registration, and we check it against their documents.

#### 3. Who can give consent

**3.1 Parent or lawful guardian.** Consent must be given by a parent, or by a lawful guardian appointed by a court or recognised by law. We may ask for proof of the relationship or guardianship.

**3.2 Verifying the guardian.** We verify that the person giving consent is an identifiable adult, using reliable identity details we already hold, identity details the guardian provides, or a recognised digital verification service such as DigiLocker, as permitted under the DPDP Rules.

**3.3 Both parents.** Where the law of India or the destination country requires the consent of both parents (for example for a passport or a minor's visa), both must give it.

#### 4. What is recorded

**4.1 Guardian record.** We record the guardian's full name, relation to the student, email address and mobile number, the date and time of consent, and the version of every document accepted.

**4.2 Confirmation email.** The guardian receives an email confirmation of the consent, with a copy of the signed Student Declaration & Consent.

#### 5. Guardian's role and responsibilities

**5.1 Contracting party.** The guardian accepts the Rule Book on behalf of the student and is responsible, together with the student, for the student's obligations, including fees and the truth of all documents (Rule Book, Clause 1.5).

**5.2 Access.** The guardian has access to the student's file on the Portal and receives copies of important messages, decisions and invoices.

**5.3 Meetings.** Counselling sessions with a student under 18 are held with a guardian present, or with the guardian's written agreement and informed of the outcome.

#### 6. Safeguarding

**6.1 Staff conduct.** Our staff must not contact a student under 18 through personal social media or personal phones, must not meet the student alone outside our office, and must not give or accept personal gifts.

**6.2 Reporting concerns.** Any concern about the safety or welfare of a student under 18 must be reported at once to the Grievance Officer, who will act and, where required, inform the guardian and the proper authorities.

#### 7. Children's data

**7.1 Protections.** We do not track, monitor the behaviour of, or send targeted advertising to students under 18. We do not send them promotional messages without the guardian's consent. Their data is used only for the Services.

#### 8. Travel and visa rules for minors

**8.1 Extra requirements.** Many countries have extra rules for students under 18, such as parental consent letters, guardianship or care arrangements in the destination country, and accommodation approved for minors. Institutions may also have their own rules. The student and guardian are responsible for meeting them. We explain the published requirements as part of the Services.

#### 9. Turning 18

**9.1 Fresh acceptance.** When a student turns 18 during the Engagement, the student will be asked to accept the Rule Book in their own name on their next login.

**9.2 Guardian access after 18.** After that, the guardian continues to have access to the file only if the student agrees (Schedule 5, Clause 6.1). Fees already due remain payable by those responsible for them.
